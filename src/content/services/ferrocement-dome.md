## Overview

Ferrocement is a thin, dense shell of cement mortar reinforced with layers of steel mesh. Formed into a dome, it produces a monolithic, column-free structure that uses a fraction of the material of an equivalent RCC building and, done properly, lasts for decades with little maintenance. It is a specialist technique — the reinforcement layering, mortar mix and application sequence all differ from conventional concrete work — and it is not something most contractors are set up to do.

This service is for clients who specifically want a dome: for a distinctive home or studio, a hard-wearing farm structure, a water tank, or a feature building where the curved form is the architecture.

## PBJ's approach

PBJ Infra treats a dome as a structural-engineering problem first. Shell thickness, mesh layers and mortar mix are designed for the span and loads, openings and services are planned into the geometry rather than cut in later, and the mortar is applied and cured in controlled layers. The Rabbit Dome House in Pune is PBJ's built reference for this work.

## Technical considerations

- **When a dome makes sense:** long clear spans, a desire for a monolithic waterproof shell, or a site where material transport is expensive — the dome's low material use is a real advantage.
- **Openings:** doors, windows and skylights are edge-reinforced and planned with the shell, since retrofitting a large opening into a stressed shell is difficult.
- **Finishing and waterproofing:** the exterior needs a considered weather coat; the interior can be plastered smooth or left textured.
- **Thermal behaviour:** the shell's mass and shape affect internal temperature — worth designing for in Maharashtra's climate.
