## Overview

Ferro concrete — more precisely ferrocement — is a thin, highly reinforced skin of cement mortar. Because the reinforcement is distributed through the whole section as fine mesh rather than concentrated in bars, the material can be formed into thin curved shells that carry load through their shape. That makes it the natural choice for domes, vaults, shell roofs, curved tanks and sculptural elements: forms that a conventional beam-and-column frame either cannot produce or can only produce at high cost and weight.

This service is for architects and clients with a specific curved or sculptural structure in mind, and for functional structures — tanks, canopies, long-span roofs — where a thin shell is simply the more efficient solution.

## PBJ's approach

PBJ Infra treats every thin-shell project as structural engineering before it is construction. The shell geometry is resolved for the spans and loads, the mesh and armature are designed layer by layer, and the mortar mix and its application sequence are specified — because with ferrocement the workmanship *is* the structure. PBJ's completed [Rabbit Dome House](/projects/rabbit-dome-house/) in Pune is the built reference for this capability.

## Technical considerations

- **Form does the work:** a thin shell is strong because of its curvature; flatten it and it needs far more material.
- **Reinforcement layering:** the number of mesh layers, their spacing and the armature bars are designed, not standardised.
- **Application:** mortar is applied and compacted in layers and cured carefully; rushing either step compromises the shell.
- **Openings and edges:** these are the stress concentrations and are detailed with extra reinforcement.
