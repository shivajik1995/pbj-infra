## Project overview

Mi Casa is a residential development at Kanakapura, on the southern edge of Bengaluru. PBJ Infra's scope was its entrance — a sculptural, free-form boundary wall with a continuous wave profile, a large circular portal ringed by a brick voussoir arch, curved flanking pillars, and the entrance gates. The form is deliberately organic, closer to the shells PBJ builds at [AKAYA Resort](/projects/akaya-resort-gokarna/) and the Rabbit Dome House than to a conventional compound wall, and it carries the development's nautilus emblem at its centre.

It draws on PBJ's [ferro concrete design](/services/ferro-concrete-design/) capability — building a curved, sculptural surface over blockwork and a shaped armature, then rendering and finishing it by hand.

## Client requirement

The developer wanted an entrance that would set Mi Casa apart from the row of conventional gated projects along Kanakapura Road — a piece of built identity rather than a boundary wall with a signboard. The brief called for a flowing, sculptural form, a strong central feature to carry the brand emblem, and gates that matched the language of the wall, all delivered quickly enough not to hold up the wider project.

## The challenge

A free-form wall has no repeating module. Every curve of the wave profile and the full circle of the central portal had to be set out on site, built up in blockwork, and trued to a smooth surface before rendering. The circular opening needed a proper arch ring — built here as a brick voussoir band — to carry the load around it. And the whole feature had to be finished to a clean, sculptural standard in about two months.

## PBJ's approach

PBJ built the wall as a shaped masonry-and-render structure rather than a cast one. The wave profile and the portal were set out full size, raised in blockwork with the circular opening formed and its brick ring turned, then coated and modelled in successive render layers to the final flowing surface. The pillars and gate framing were built into the wall line, and the finish was carried out in two tones — white across the field, ochre on the raised forms and the portal ring — to read the way the design intended.

## Construction / execution

Site clearing and setting out; foundations and the blockwork wall following the wave profile; the circular portal formed with its brick voussoir arch; flanking pillars and gate posts; then render in layers, the two-tone finish, and the hanging of the entrance gates. The full scope ran to roughly two months.

## Final result

A finished sculptural entrance for Mi Casa — a single flowing wall with a circular portal at its heart and matching gates — built and finished by PBJ Infra in about two months, and a compact example of the company's free-form structural work away from its pools and domes.
